Purpose

Products should help people act on their ideas.

We build affordable, privacy-first, buy-once products and use them in real workflows and finished work. The point is not software for its own sake. The point is helping someone become more capable, creative, and independent.

That means reducing repetitive work without hiding the process, keeping ownership with the person doing the work, and making the judgment that remains after automation visible and valuable.

What we build

We make focused Windows products for work and play: practical apps, games that reward skill and strategy, and eventually small widgets that earn their place. They should be understandable, buy-once, and useful without a subscription-shaped gate.

Different products have different jobs, but the standard remains the same: give people more control over their files, attention, choices, and time.

What we make

We also make books, photography, and other Works. They are not a pile of generated output. They are shaped through selection, rejection, editing, sequencing, and care.

AI can help create options, but people create the result. Taste, curation, and responsibility belong to the person who decides what stays, what changes, and what is ready to share.

Why 3thousand30

3thousand30 is a reminder to build around principles that still matter in the future: ownership, privacy, useful craft, and room for new ideas. Make products people can keep, market them honestly, and leave people with more control than they started with.

What we stand for

Operating beliefs for products, play, and the work that follows.

1. Empowerment over extraction

A product should give the user leverage, not turn attention, data, or dependence into the real business model.

2. Ownership over dependency

Buy-once pricing and portable local files let the workflow continue even when a subscription would otherwise become a gate.

3. Privacy as a principle

If a tool can work locally, it should. If something must leave the machine, the user should know what, where, and why.

4. Transparency and choice

AI-enabled apps let users choose providers, models, and cost profiles instead of hiding those decisions behind one opaque service.

5. The person owns the result

AI can create options. Human review, rejection, correction, curation, and selection create the result—and the responsibility for it stays human.

6. Capability building

The best workflow or game leaves the person better able to repeat, adapt, understand, or enjoy the experience next time.

7. Works reflect the standard

Books, photography, and other Works should show care, useful ideas, and a point of view—not simply demonstrate that a model can produce output.

8. Organic growth over heavy advertising

Useful products, honest use cases, and finished Works should do more of the convincing than manufactured urgency.

Product principles

What every product should try to preserve.

  1. Reduce dependency rather than increase it.
  2. Stay simple enough for ordinary people to begin.
  3. Help users keep ownership of their work, play, files, and results.
  4. Respect privacy by default and avoid unnecessary cloud reliance.
  5. Make any external data flow obvious.
  6. Support provider and workflow choice whenever practical.
  7. Stay accessible in price and useful without subscription lock-in.
  8. Treat free product updates as the normal relationship, not an upsell trap.
  9. Help the user become more capable over time.
  10. When the product is a game, reward skill, strategy, and attention.

Where we stand

Small products can still make a serious difference.

We are not trying to build an everything-platform or trap every workflow inside one account. We are building focused products, practical systems, games, and real Works for people who value ownership, privacy, affordability, and judgment.

Own the product. Shape the result. Keep the work.

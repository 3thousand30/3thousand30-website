Reduce Scanned PDFs for Email and Archiving

Use Flatten to reduce scan-heavy PDFs, then compare readability and file size before email or archive use.

real_life_scenario

Scanned receipts, correspondence, case files, or reference packs contain large JPEG images and exceed an email limit or consume unnecessary archive space.

finished_outcome

A reviewed set of scan-heavy PDF copies with documented before/after sizes and legible text, signatures, stamps, and small details.

BCP Batch Compress PDF

step_by_step

The working process

  1. 1

    Identify scan-heavy candidates

    Choose PDFs whose pages are mainly photographed or scanned images. Do not assume a large text-native PDF benefits from the same settings.

  2. 2

    Record the baseline

    Note source sizes, page counts, and the practical email or archive target. Size reduction has meaning only relative to a real constraint.

  3. 3

    Test Flatten on a representative file

    In Batch Compress PDF, select Flatten for the strongest reduction on scan-heavy PDFs. Preview pages containing fine print, handwriting, stamps, and low-contrast regions before running the full folder.

  4. 4

    Compare readability, not only percentage

    Zoom to normal reading size and to the scale used for verification. Reject settings that blur account numbers, signatures, dates, diagrams, or evidentiary detail.

  5. 5

    Run and inspect the measured results

    Process the compatible set, then compare input and output sizes. Investigate unchanged or unexpectedly large results instead of assuming every scan should shrink.

  6. 6

    Validate the delivery or archive copy

    Open several outputs in the intended viewer, confirm page count and orientation, and keep the high-quality source archive when the reduced file is only a delivery derivative.

human_checkpoint

Review before you call it finished

  • □Are fine print and handwriting still legible?
  • □Are page order, rotation, and geometry unchanged?
  • □Does the measured size meet the real constraint?
  • □Are archival masters kept when reduced files are delivery copies?

honest_boundaries

Limits and privacy

Practical limitation

Lossy image recompression can soften detail. It cannot recover a poor scan, and a reduced delivery copy may not satisfy archival or evidentiary retention requirements.

Privacy boundary

Scans are decoded, compared, and written locally through the bundled PDF engines. Nothing is uploaded by the app.

faq

Questions worth answering

Will OCR text remain?+

Flatten creates page-image copies and should not be used when OCR/searchability must remain intact. Use Smart for structure-preserving compression and verify searchability on representative outputs.

Is the smallest file always best?+

No. Readability and retention requirements matter more than a headline compression percentage.
